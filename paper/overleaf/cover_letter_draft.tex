\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider our manuscript, ``Grid decarbonization changes the lifecycle
value and evidence requirements of data-center cooling,'' for
publication as a research article in \emph{Applied Energy}.

The study addresses an energy-system decision that is increasingly important
for high-density computing: whether the lifecycle advantage of a cooling
architecture transfers across electricity systems and through grid
decarbonization. We independently reconstruct 24 released hyperscale
data-center results and combine them with nine EPA eGRID releases comprising
459 state-year observations. The analysis identifies a grid-dependent
cold-plate/one-phase crossover, quantifies the experimental discrimination
needed to resolve close rankings, and shows that absolute avoided emissions
contract while embodied importance rises as electricity decarbonizes.
Two-phase immersion remains lowest under all observed state-years and a broad
server-inventory stress range, while the manuscript explicitly identifies the
foreground assumptions that limit transferability.

The methodological contribution is an open, versioned framework that connects
cooling performance, hourly integration, reliability, lifecycle inventories,
provenance, and comparative-claim checks. All code and redistributable derived
results are publicly available. The work therefore bridges energy-system
modeling, data-center thermal management, lifecycle assessment, and practical
decision support.

This manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has
been approved by all authors. [Confirm before submission.] We believe it fits
\emph{Applied Energy}'s emphasis on energy efficiency, energy-system
decarbonization, data analytics, and translation from analysis to
implementation.
