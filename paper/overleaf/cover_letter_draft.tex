\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider our manuscript, ``Transferability audit of data-center cooling
LCA: lifecycle electricity, functional equivalence, and rank robustness,'' as a
Technology Assessment in \emph{Applied Energy}.

The paper examines whether a released hyperscale cooling assessment can support
decisions outside its original electricity cases. We reproduce its 24 published
totals, audit the archived endpoint formulas, harmonize nine EPA eGRID releases,
and calculate 71 at-user electricity systems from the 2023 Federal LCA Commons
baseline. The archive audit identifies an unresolved GWP100/GTP100 endpoint
identity. The national Federal result is 422.93 kg CO2e/MWh, but 287 unlinked
technosphere inputs and provider-scope infrastructure omissions make it a
partial linked-system factor rather than a complete electricity benchmark.

The paper also replaces unequal ad hoc stress widths with exact equal-width
rank-reversal bounds. At the national electricity factor, the numerical first
rank reverses at 2.64\% service-equivalence variation, 2.99\% use-phase
variation, and 1.32\% when all blocks vary. These are deterministic
transferability limits, not probabilities and not evidence that one cooling
architecture is environmentally superior.

The contribution is therefore a reproducible claim-boundary method: it shows
which published conclusions can be reconstructed, which transfers are
numerically fragile, and which primary data are required for a public
comparative assertion. Code, derived tables, cutoff records, and execution
metadata accompany the manuscript. A tagged immutable archive and the authors'
final declarations will be completed before submission.

Please note that this is a working cover-letter draft. The authors must confirm
originality, authorship, competing interests, funding, and approval before it is
used for submission.

\closing{Sincerely,\\Han Hu}
